The default success document of a read endpoint whose `include` option is `true` or a `Query.IncludeOptions` now admits in `included` every resource its legal include paths reach, beside the non-`paginated` direct targets it admitted before (#320). Under `include: true`, `Endpoint.get(Article, { include: true })` advertises `comments.author`, and its document now carries the `people` that path brings in: a handler returning them type-checks, and `GET /articles/1?include=comments.author` answers 200 where it failed to encode and answered 500. The walk is bounded as the paths are: two hops under `include: true`, the `depth` an option names, or the exact `paths` of an allow-list. The same holds for `Endpoint.list`, `related` (over the target's graph), `collection` and `polymorphic` (over every member's graph), and the `get` / `list` / related endpoints `Endpoint.resource` and `Group.resource` derive.

An endpoint with `include` off, or with a declared `Include.Surface`, keeps exactly the document it had, on the wire and in its type.

A widened `include: boolean` (say `include: flag` with `flag: boolean`) is typed as admitting what either value admits, since the endpoint built from it may have turned `include` on and then binds the full walk at runtime. This holds on every read endpoint, `collection` and `polymorphic` included (#382), so for a document without `fields` their client-side `included` type is no narrower than what the server sends. Under `fields: true` the reached resources are admitted only in strict form for now: the sparse fieldset types do not yet distribute over a widened `include` (#397). An `include` that may be a declared surface or `true` (`which ? surface : true`) widens likewise on `collection` and `polymorphic`: it is typed as either the surface's own union or the full walk the endpoint binds when it is `true`, so a handler returning the `people` that `comments.author` brings in type-checks.

The widened union is visible to clients too, since it is what `HttpApiClient` decodes `included` into and what the OpenAPI output describes: a client regenerated from the OpenAPI document sees the new member types, and an exhaustive `switch` on `included[].type` needs an arm for each of them, under a widened `include: boolean` as well as `include: true`. `Group.resource` turns this on for its derived `get`, `list` and related endpoints by default, since it defaults to `include: true`.
